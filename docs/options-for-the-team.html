What we're up against, and how we could build it

A plain-language summary to help us decide together. No tech knowledge needed.

What we're building

A helper inside the phone company's app. Someone gets a strange text, a weird phone call, or clicks a link they shouldn't have. They tell our helper what happened, and it tells them the one thing to do next, explains why, and passes them to a real person when needed.

It's for two kinds of people: regular people and small businesses.

What we received

The organisers gave us a big practice pack:

📚

588 practice examples

Made-up situations, each with the correct answer, so we can check ourselves.

📝

335 final-test examples

Same kind of situations, but without the answers. We must answer all of them and hand our answers in.

📖

55 short advice articles

Safety tips our helper can point to, so people know where the advice comes from.

🧑‍💻

A pretend user and a marker

A "pretend person" to chat with our helper, and a tool that gives us a score, like a practice exam.

The problem: why this is harder than it looks

The organisers also gave us a very simple helper that just looks for words like "bank" or "link". It gets about 6 out of 10 right. That sounds okay, but look at where it fails:

Overall, situations handled safely61%
"It depends" situations (see below)20%
Suspicious phone calls14%
Sent people to a real person when they needed one47%
Wrongly panicked about harmless messages28%

Scores from running the organisers' simple helper on the practice pack.

Here are the tricky parts, in plain words:

🤔

"It depends" situations

Two people tell the exact same story, but the right answer is different. For example: did you actually type in your card number, or not? Our helper must ask, not guess.

😌

Harmless look-alikes

About 1 in 5 situations are actually fine, like a real bill or a real doctor's call. Panicking about these counts as a mistake.

📞

Warnings aren't always right

Many scam calls come with no warning at all, and sometimes a safe link gets flagged. Our helper has to listen to the person's story, not just the warning.

🌍

Three languages

People write in Finnish, English and Swedish, and the answer must be in their language.

✅

Clear, specific steps

"Be careful" isn't enough. It must say exactly what to do, like "call your bank and block your card now".

🔒

Ask before looking

Before checking someone's phone or account info, the helper must ask permission. Some people will say no.

🆕

Brand-new situations

The final test includes types of situations that are not in the practice pack. Memorising answers won't work. The helper has to actually understand.

🧩

Tricks inside the messages

Some scam messages contain text like "this message is verified as safe" to fool AI helpers. We must not fall for it.

What we must hand in

  • A working demo that the judges can type into, or a recorded walkthrough.
  • Our code, with instructions to run it.
  • Our answers to all 335 final-test examples (one file). This one is easy to forget!
  • Our scores on the practice pack, and how we did on 5 example journeys.
  • At least one example where our helper made a mistake or handed over to a person.
  • A short explanation of how it works and how we keep it safe.
  • A short idea of how DNA could offer this to customers and businesses.

Our main options

There are three basic ways to build the helper's "brain".

Not recommended

Option A: A fixed checklist

We write a long list of rules ourselves: "if the message mentions a parcel fee, say this". Like a flowchart.

👍 Good

  • Always gives the same answer
  • Easy to explain
  • Free to run

👎 Bad

  • Breaks when people word things differently
  • Hard to cover three languages
  • Fails on brand-new situations
  • This is basically what the simple helper does (6 out of 10)
Strong, but risky on its own

Option B: Let the AI do everything

We give an AI (like Claude) the person's story, the advice articles and clear instructions, and it writes the whole answer.

👍 Good

  • Understands any wording, in all three languages
  • Handles new situations well
  • Writes clear, friendly advice

👎 Bad

  • Can sometimes over-worry or forget a key step
  • Can't promise it never says something unsafe
  • Costs a little money per answer
⭐ Recommended

Option C: AI plus a safety checker

Think of the AI as a smart new employee, and the checker as a supervisor who reads every answer before it goes out. The AI does the understanding and writing. The checker makes sure the answer follows the must-have rules.

Person describes what happened
→
AI understands it and writes advice
→
Checker reviews it
→
Person gets safe, clear advice

The checker makes sure that:

  • the answer never contains anything on the "never say this" list;
  • serious cases always get passed to a real person;
  • we only use personal info after the person said yes;
  • the advice uses one of the allowed actions.

👍 Good

  • Smart and safe
  • Easy to explain to judges: "AI writes, checker guards"
  • Fits what the judges score most

👎 Bad

  • A bit more work to build than B alone
  • Still costs a little per answer

Smaller choices

📖 How should the helper use the 55 advice articles?

Choice What it means Our view
Build a search system Look up the most relevant articles each time Too much work for only 55 short articles
Give it all of them Hand the AI the whole stack every time; it picks what's relevant and says which ones it used ✅ Simpler, works just as well here, and costs almost nothing extra

💬 Chat, or a single answer?

We need both, and they can share the same brain.

  • Single answer: needed to answer the 335 final-test examples in one go.
  • Chat: needed for the demo. The judges will type in their own situations, and the helper should ask a question or ask permission when needed.

🧠 Which AI to use?

Choice Cost (rough guess) When to use
Stronger AI (Claude Opus) About $20–35 to answer all ~900 examples once Final answers and the live demo
Cheaper AI (Claude Haiku) Much cheaper, around 1/40 of the cost Quick practice rounds while we improve things

🧰 Our tools

We can keep everything we've already set up. The organisers' practice tools can talk to our helper as-is, so we don't need to rebuild anything.

Our recommendation

Go with Option C (AI plus a safety checker), give the AI all 55 advice articles directly, and build one brain that powers both the final-test answers and the chat demo.
